River of Life: Blood donations in Australia and their support to the healthcare system

Blood donations are a crucial pillar of the Australian healthcare
system. Many medical cases from surgeries to blood-related conditions rely
on donors to supply blood for the patients.

This will be examined through the realities of blood donors,
how the blood is used, and the logistics of blood products

Blood Donation Demographic

African-born Australians are known for their willingness to help others,
but often turn down blood donations due to perceived fears
of discrimination and distrust towards the donation system.

This can be oberserved with the majority not being blood donors
regardless of whether they have seen the materials or not.

It is also observed from the chart that the proportion of
members who have seen and not seen the campaign materials
is roughly half each.

Among the group, Eastern Africa makes up a large portion, while
Northern Africa has more people who has seen the materials than
those who have not seen it. Christians and University or TAFE-goers
are also a majority of the group.

Hence, Informational campaigns regarding the realities of blood donation are
necessary to continue dispelling the perception and encourage African-born Australians
to become participants. Donor centers will also need to promote
inclusivity and transparency to encourage african donors to participate.

The next largest factors are medication, especially involving immunosuppresants

For Permanent deferral, which appears to be rarer, extreme cases like
Chronic and organ conditions and even infection and prion diseases

Blood product demand and supply

Immunoglobulin patients are observed to increase steadily across the years.
The states with the largest amount of patients are
New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland, signifying a growing demand
for immunoglobulin.

Throughout the years, demand for red blood cells, platelets and Fresh
Frozen Plasma has fluctuated greatly among states.

For red blood cells, South Australia consistently had the highest issuances,
while for Platelets, Queensland had a constant high issuance. Fresh
Frozen Plasma consistently fluctuated in demand across all states.

Northern territory is the only state that consistently has a
low demand for issuances of blood products, given that their
population has roughly the same blood type allowing healthcare services
to reliably obtain the necessary amount of blood prodcts for
treatment(Gemini,2026).

Despite fluctuation in issuance for each blood product across all
the states from 2015 to 2025, Red Blood Cells are consistently the
product with the highest issuance, given their use in many medical settings.
Platelets are second in demand, having a higher issuance than
Fresh Frozen Plasma(FFP) due to their extensive use in specific
medical procedures and lower shelf life, requiring continuous reissuance(Gemini, 2026).

Red blood cells demand climbs steadily over the years
highlighting the increasing demand for it

Platelets have a sharp increase in demand around 2021
to 2022, possibly due to an increase in medical procedures requiring its use.

Fresh Frozen Plasma has experienced a sharp decline over
the years, given that alternative clotting factor concentrates
have more advantages over it (Gemini,2026)

Immunoglobulin dispensed in 2024-25: from medical condition to product (grams)

Immunoglobin is used to develop products like Privegen AU that
treat conditions of low antibodies like hypogammaglobulinemia,

Blood Donation Logistics

New South Wales is shown to have the highest amount
of treatment centers, with a total of 8, 2 from
newcastle, 5 from sydney and 1 from Canberra.

The Northern Territory and Tasmania only have 1 treatment center.

The spending for blood products is shown to increase from
2020 to 2025

It is also observed that Fresh Blood Products is the
highest spent product while Imported Ig is shown to gradually
increase in spending as well.

For all states in the year 2024-25, it is shown
that Immunoglobin supply relies more on imports, with domestic production
being only 42.5%

South Australia, Western Australia and Tasmania's private hospitals show
to have more domestic immunoglobin than imports, while Northern Territory
is shown to lack any private hospitals and rely more
on public, heavily relying on imports of immunoglobin.

This may hint to a need to increase production of
domestic immunoglobin and reduce reliance on imports,
especially for Northern Territory.

As seen from the immunoglobin supply chart, the issuance
of blood products across states also lean heavily to imported
products, as the supply itself is more on imports rather
than domestic production.

If international imports were to drop or halt, Australia may
face an immunoglobin shortage in states like Northern territory or
Australian Capital territory due to lacking their major source of
immunoglobin.

It can be observed that along the years,
Red Blood Cells discard rate is getting lesser for each state,
showing that initiative is being taken to preserve the blood
supply and prevent wastage

However, platelets and Fresh Frozen Plasma discard rate remain high,
especially for more remote areas of Australia. This may be
due to those areas lacking the necessary equipment to preserve
the products.

Conclusion

In conclusion, It is shown that blood donations play a crucial role
in the Australian healthcare, especially with how its demands rises
across all states and years.

To ensure that blood donation campaigns can be more successful, blood
donation centers can be more inclusive and launch campaigns to educate
minorities, especially African-born Australian on the realities of Australian
Blood Donation and reassure them that their blood will be well used through
transperency.

While accidents may also happen, they are rare and decreasing every year that
blood donation can be reassured to be a safe activity.

We have also observed that there is a growing demand for blood products and how
they are used to treat patients with different ailments, but also seen how much
Australia's healthcare relies on imports of Immunoglobin, and why it may be important
for them to consider manufacturing domestic supplies. We have also observed Australia's
healthcare spending and the amount of supplies discarded.

Although Australia has made efforts to reduce the waste, the equipment and
infrastructure gap remains a challenge in preserving supplies and reducing
discards.
Henceforth, Blood donations remain a crucial pillar to Australia's healthcare
and should always strive to improve for the coming years, be it in donation
campaigns or supply-handling equipment.
